Part I: Bayesian Foundations

Core Philosophy

"Probability quantifies uncertainty about parameters, not just long-run frequencies"

Bayes' Theorem

$$P(\theta | \mathbf{x}) = \frac{P(\mathbf{x} | \theta) P(\theta)}{P(\mathbf{x})}$$

Components:

  • $P(\theta | \mathbf{x})$: Posterior
  • $P(\mathbf{x} | \theta)$: Likelihood
  • $P(\theta)$: Prior
  • $P(\mathbf{x})$: Evidence
Interpretation:
"How should I update my beliefs about $\theta$ after observing data $\mathbf{x}$?"

Why Bayesian Methods?

AdvantageDescriptionExample
Uncertainty Quantification Direct probability statements "95% probability μ ∈ [1.2, 1.8]"
Prior Knowledge Incorporate domain expertise Historical data, expert opinion
Small Sample Performance Effective with limited data Clinical trials, rare events
Hierarchical Modeling Natural parameter sharing Multi-level data structures

Part II: Conjugate Priors

Normal Distribution Analysis

Normal-Normal Conjugacy (Known Variance)

Setup:

$$X_i \stackrel{iid}{\sim} N(\mu, \sigma^2)$$ $$\mu \sim N(\mu_0, \tau_0^2)$$

Posterior:

$$\mu | \mathbf{x} \sim N(\mu_n, \tau_n^2)$$

Posterior Parameters:

$$\tau_n^{-2} = \tau_0^{-2} + n\sigma^{-2}$$ $$\mu_n = \frac{\tau_0^{-2}\mu_0 + n\sigma^{-2}\bar{x}}{\tau_0^{-2} + n\sigma^{-2}}$$

Normal Distribution: Key Insights

Precision Weighting

  • Posterior mean is precision-weighted average
  • More precise information gets higher weight
  • As $n \to \infty$: $\mu_n \to \bar{x}$ (data dominates)

Practical Considerations

  • Prior choice: Affects finite-sample inference
  • Computational advantage: Analytical solution
  • Interpretability: Conjugacy preserves distribution family

Example: Drug Efficacy Study

Scenario

New drug reduces blood pressure. Historical studies suggest mean reduction = 12 mmHg (σ = 3).

Bayesian Analysis

  • Prior: μ ~ N(12, 2²) [moderately confident]
  • Data: n = 20 patients, x̄ = 10.5 mmHg
  • Likelihood: σ = 3 (assumed known)
$$\tau_n^{-2} = 2^{-2} + 20 \cdot 3^{-2} = 0.25 + 2.22 = 2.47$$ $$\mu_n = \frac{0.25 \cdot 12 + 2.22 \cdot 10.5}{2.47} = 10.7$$

Result: Posterior μ | x ~ N(10.7, 0.64²)

Poisson-Gamma Conjugacy

Count Data Model:

$$X_i \stackrel{iid}{\sim} \text{Poisson}(\lambda)$$ $$\lambda \sim \text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta)$$

Posterior:

$$\lambda | \mathbf{x} \sim \text{Gamma}(\alpha_n, \beta_n)$$

Update Rules:

$$\alpha_n = \alpha + \sum_{i=1}^n x_i$$ $$\beta_n = \beta + n$$

Interpretation: Add observed counts to prior shape, add observations to prior rate

Poisson Applications

Common Use Cases:

  • Hospital emergency arrivals
  • Software defects per module
  • Network packet arrivals
  • Gene mutations per sequence

Key Properties:

  • Natural for count data
  • Rate parameter λ > 0
  • Mean = Variance = λ
  • Memoryless property

Example: Website Traffic Analysis

Problem

E-commerce site wants to model hourly visitor arrivals. Historical data suggests λ ≈ 50 visitors/hour.

Bayesian Setup

  • Prior: λ ~ Gamma(100, 2) [E[λ] = 50, Var[λ] = 25]
  • Data: 10 hours of observation: {45, 52, 48, 55, 42, 49, 53, 47, 51, 58}
$$\alpha_n = 100 + (45 + 52 + \cdots + 58) = 100 + 500 = 600$$ $$\beta_n = 2 + 10 = 12$$ $$\lambda | \mathbf{x} \sim \text{Gamma}(600, 12)$$

Posterior mean: 600/12 = 50, Posterior std: √(600)/12 ≈ 2.04

Posterior Predictive Distribution

Prediction for Future Observations

For Poisson-Gamma, the posterior predictive is Negative Binomial:

$$P(X_{new} = k | \mathbf{x}) = \binom{k + \alpha_n - 1}{k} \left(\frac{\beta_n}{\beta_n + 1}\right)^{\alpha_n} \left(\frac{1}{\beta_n + 1}\right)^k$$

Practical Advantage

Unlike point estimates, provides full uncertainty quantification for predictions

Part III: Modern Approximation Methods

Variational Inference

The Intractability Problem

When Conjugacy Breaks Down

  • Complex hierarchical models
  • Non-conjugate priors
  • Mixture models
  • Neural networks with Bayesian weights
$$P(\theta | \mathbf{x}) = \frac{P(\mathbf{x} | \theta) P(\theta)}{P(\mathbf{x})} = \frac{P(\mathbf{x} | \theta) P(\theta)}{\int P(\mathbf{x} | \theta) P(\theta) d\theta}$$

The integral in the denominator is often analytically intractable

Variational Inference Approach

Core Idea

Approximate intractable posterior with simpler distribution from variational family

$$q^*(\theta) = \arg\min_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \text{KL}[q(\theta) || p(\theta | \mathbf{x})]$$

Advantages:

  • Deterministic optimization
  • Faster than MCMC
  • Scales to large datasets
  • Easy to parallelize

Limitations:

  • Underestimates uncertainty
  • Approximate solution only
  • Local optima issues
  • Family choice critical

Evidence Lower BOund (ELBO)

$$\log p(\mathbf{x}) = \text{ELBO}(q) + \text{KL}[q(\theta) || p(\theta | \mathbf{x})]$$
$$\text{ELBO}(q) = \mathbb{E}_q[\log p(\mathbf{x}, \theta)] - \mathbb{E}_q[\log q(\theta)]$$ $$= \mathbb{E}_q[\log p(\mathbf{x} | \theta)] - \text{KL}[q(\theta) || p(\theta)]$$

Key Insight

Since KL ≥ 0, maximizing ELBO minimizes KL divergence to true posterior

Mean Field Variational Family

Independence Assumption

$$q(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^d q_i(\theta_i)$$

Assume posterior factorizes into independent marginals

Coordinate Ascent VI (CAVI)

$$q_j^*(\theta_j) \propto \exp\left(\mathbb{E}_{q_{-j}}[\log p(\mathbf{x}, \theta)]\right)$$

Update each factor holding others fixed until convergence

VI Example: Bayesian Linear Regression

Model

$$y_i = \mathbf{x}_i^T \boldsymbol{\beta} + \epsilon_i, \quad \epsilon_i \sim N(0, \sigma^2)$$ $$\boldsymbol{\beta} \sim N(\mathbf{0}, \lambda^{-1}\mathbf{I})$$

True Posterior:

Conjugate → Analytical

$$\boldsymbol{\beta} | \mathbf{y} \sim N(\boldsymbol{\mu}_n, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_n)$$

VI Approximation:

Mean field factorization

$$q(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \prod_{j=1}^p N(\mu_j, \sigma_j^2)$$

Result: VI underestimates posterior correlations but provides fast approximation

ApplicationModel TypeVI Advantage

Topic Modeling (LDA) Dirichlet-multinomial Handles millions of documents
Bayesian Neural Networks Weight uncertainty Tractable training at scale

Spike and Slab Methods

Bayesian Variable Selection

The Variable Selection Problem

High-Dimensional Challenge

Given regression $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ with $p$ predictors:

  • Which variables are truly relevant?
  • How to quantify selection uncertainty?
  • Traditional methods (stepwise, LASSO) lack uncertainty quantification

Applications

  • Genomics: Which genes affect disease?
  • Finance: Which factors predict returns?
  • Marketing: Which features drive customer behavior?

Spike and Slab Prior

Two-Component Mixture

$$\beta_j | \gamma_j \sim (1 - \gamma_j) \delta_0 + \gamma_j N(0, \tau^2)$$ $$\gamma_j \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\pi)$$

Spike Component (δ₀):

  • Point mass at zero
  • Variable excluded from model
  • Probability: (1 - γⱼ)

Slab Component (N(0,τ²)):

  • Diffuse normal distribution
  • Variable included in model
  • Probability: γⱼ

Posterior Inference

$$P(\gamma_j = 1 | \mathbf{y}) = \text{Posterior Inclusion Probability}$$

Interpretation

  • P(γⱼ = 1 | y) > 0.5: Variable likely relevant
  • P(γⱼ = 1 | y) < 0.1: Variable likely irrelevant
  • 0.1 < P(γⱼ = 1 | y) < 0.5: Uncertain

Computational Challenge

2^p possible models → MCMC sampling required for inference

Example: Gene Expression Analysis

Scenario

Predict cancer outcome from 1000 gene expression levels (p = 1000, n = 100)

Spike and Slab Setup

  • Prior inclusion probability: π = 0.01 (expect ~10 relevant genes)
  • Slab variance: τ² = 1 (moderate effect sizes)
  • MCMC: Gibbs sampling for 10,000 iterations
$$\text{Selected genes} = \{j : P(\gamma_j = 1 | \mathbf{y}) > 0.5\}$$

Method Comparison

MethodUncertaintySpeedInterpretability
Spike & Slab ✅ Full posterior ❌ Slow (MCMC) ✅ Clear probabilities
LASSO ❌ Point estimates ✅ Fast optimization ⚠️ Arbitrary threshold
Random Forest ⚠️ Importance scores ✅ Fast ❌ Black box
Elastic Net ❌ Point estimates ✅ Fast ⚠️ Two hyperparameters

Spike and Slab Variants

Continuous Spike & Slab:

$$\beta_j \sim (1-\pi)N(0,\tau_0^2) + \pi N(0,\tau_1^2)$$

τ₀² ≪ τ₁² (no exact zeros)

Horseshoe Prior:

$$\beta_j \sim N(0, \lambda_j^2 \tau^2)$$ $$\lambda_j \sim \text{Half-Cauchy}(1)$$

Heavy-tailed continuous shrinkage

Trade-offs

  • Discrete spike & slab: Clear interpretation, harder computation
  • Continuous variants: Easier computation, less interpretable

Part IV: Applications & Conclusions

When to Use Each Method

ScenarioRecommended MethodRationale
Small sample, strong prior knowledge Conjugate priors (Normal/Poisson) Exact inference, interpretable

Real-time inference required Variational Inference Fast, no sampling variability

Key Takeaways

  1. Conjugate priors provide exact, interpretable inference for fundamental models
  2. Variational inference trades accuracy for computational efficiency in complex models
  3. Spike & slab methods offer principled uncertainty quantification for variable selection
  4. Method choice depends on data size, model complexity, and computational constraints
  5. Bayesian framework unifies uncertainty quantification across diverse applications
